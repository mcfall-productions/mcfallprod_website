YouTube pricing is bespoke, because how much of a channel's back catalog needs work varies a lot. The prices below are where we start, and we'll set the final quote on a walkthrough call, once we understand the client's channel.

What's covered: retitling, show notes and transcripts for the whole back catalog, published for the client on their podcast host and website, with every original saved so any episode can be put back. YouTube is added on top, as described below.

There are two review levels. With Reviewed, our tool drafts and a US-based native English speaker on our team reads every title and set of show notes. Guest names and their books or offers are checked in every transcript. With Automated, we use the same tool and the same publishing, but the copy goes through automated checks instead of a full read: guest and book names against a list we build with the client, a scan for outdated or sensitive language, and length and duplicate checks. We spot-check a sample of every batch and read anything flagged.

YouTube add-ons, price per episode, starting at:

| | Reviewed | Automated |
|---|---|---|
| Titles and descriptions that mirror the audio feed | $2 | $2 |
| Titles and descriptions written separately for YouTube | $12 | $5 |

Mirrored copy costs almost nothing extra, because the copy already exists and adding it to YouTube is automated. Separate YouTube copy costs more at either level. Reviewed adds a second read, since YouTube titles and descriptions need their own review apart from the audio feed ones. Automated still means our tool creates the new copy and puts it on YouTube. Transcripts on YouTube are automated too, so they add very little.

Thumbnails can be included for an additional cost. We'd set that price based on catalog size and what the client expects when it's done.

For comparison, freelancers charge roughly $25 to $150 for one set of show notes, and AI transcription alone runs about $15 for an hour-long episode, with no speaker labels.

For a 500-episode catalog, the Full Suite takes about 2 weeks Reviewed and 1 to 2 weeks Automated. A catalog of 1,000 to 2,000 episodes takes a few more weeks, not months. YouTube timing depends on how much of the channel a client wants done, so we'll confirm it after the call.

The Full Suite also includes three months of monthly tune-ups, a written style guide for the client's team, and a 60-day comparison of the episodes we changed against the ones we left alone, at no charge.

Everything starts with a free audit. We crawl the blog, the audio feed and, if they want, YouTube. We don't need any logins. The client gets a plain list of what's wrong, and the quote comes after a walkthrough call.

We're promising discoverability, not a guaranteed lift in downloads. We don't have clean before and after numbers yet.
