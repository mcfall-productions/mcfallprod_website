## Key takeaways
- Richard works mostly web-video first; YouTube is the discoverability engine (titles = search, thumbnails = home feed). Audio is more of a "captive audience" of followers.
- Hard sell is convincing people to pay for retroactive cleanup. He wants data. Without it, lean on the fear angle ("do you know what your titles said five years ago?") and cleanup of outdated or embarrassing language.
- Suggested offering it YouTube-forward too (title, description, transcripts), though YouTube is a more competitive market and thumbnails are a separate job.
- Schema markup on show-notes pages so LLMs can read them was new to Scott and Lauren. Likely backdated almost nowhere.
- Multilingual: YouTube lets you add translated titles, descriptions and thumbnails per language; Ditto Dub does dubbing in the host's own voice (about $48/month for 30 min; he saw $194 for 3 hours). Uploading corrected transcripts avoids mis-heard words. Not an audio-platform feature yet.
- Richard's advice: don't oversell the human oversight. Clients want results; sell the data and process. Fair concern about AI content being detected and downranked.
- US-based English-speaking reviewers is a selling point (95% of their clients are American).
- Relationship-building matters more than outreach; Richard is not the buyer but knows people who are.
- Scott earlier had a calculator on the site (copywriter at $25/hr, 500 sets of show notes: about 4 months and $100,000 by hand vs $40,000 and 2 weeks with McFall). It has since been taken down.

## What McFall said about the offer
- Origin: School of Greatness (SOG) retitling and transcript project. Simplecast has about 2,000 episodes; the last 1,500 exist twice (ad and ad-free), so about 4,000 titles by hand.
- A custom tool learns the host's voice and outputs three titles per episode from the old title, show notes and transcript. A human (Scott and Lauren) reviews and picks every title.
- Services: retitle, show notes, transcripts (guest names and their books/offers spelled correctly), blog (WordPress) with a "click to read transcript" button, all consistent across host, Apple, Spotify and blog.
- Promise is discoverability, not a guaranteed revenue lift. No hard numbers yet. SOG was retitled 100% with no holdout, so there is no control group.
- Revenue logic: dynamic ad insertion pays on every play, so more plays of old episodes means more ad money.
- Show notes review by a human takes 3 to 4 minutes each; "weeks, not months" even for 1,000 to 2,000 episodes.
- Funnel: free audit with no logins (crawls blog, audio feed, optionally YouTube), then pitch the full suite or à la carte.
- Referral partners: 15% commission to whoever sends an intro email that results in a sale.
- Idea: with a client's download history, show the share of listening that goes to the back catalog and aim to lift it.
- YouTube analytics for smaller past clients were full of bot views, so they have never tested titles on clean data.
